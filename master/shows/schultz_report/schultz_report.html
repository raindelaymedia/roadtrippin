How the grades work

The short version: every YouTube upload is compared with The Schultz Report's own recent uploads of the same kind, at the same age. A = doing better than about 80% of them, C = about average, F = behind about 80% of them. It's a comparison with ourselves, not with other channels.

Like for like. Episodes vs episodes, clips vs clips, Shorts vs Shorts. A 3-day-old clip is compared with how other clips were doing on their day 3, so new uploads aren't punished for being new. The comparison group is the same kind of upload from the 90 days before (at least 5 needed — until then the grade shows "—").

Grade status. Provisional (under 7 days old) — an early read, likely to move. Working (7–28 days) — mostly settled. Final (28+ days) — locked at its day-28 reading, so uploads from any period compare on the same yardstick.

Five measures, each ranked within the comparison group and combined: % viewed 35% (how much people watch) · click-through rate 35% (how often a thumbnail gets clicked; for Shorts, the share of views where people kept watching instead of swiping) · views 15% · engagement 10% (likes, comments, shares per view) · subscribers per 1,000 views 5%.

Grade bands: A 80+ · B 60–80 · C 40–60 · D 20–40 · F under 20 (combined score out of 100). A measure with no data yet (e.g. too few impressions for CTR) is left out and the rest re-weighted.

Organic only: views from YouTube ads are left out of every measure (shown in the table for reference). YouTube's data runs about two days behind.
